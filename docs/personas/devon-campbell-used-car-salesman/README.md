## Story

Devon's parents came from Jamaica in the 1970s, and he grew up in Malvern. He has sold cars on
Kingston Road since he was 32, on commission, for an owner he respects and who is not a
Christian. He is registered to sell vehicles in Ontario, and by the rules of that registration he
sells only through the dealership that employs him.

He knows what people think of his trade. He has spent twelve years being the exception: telling a
single mother which car on the lot not to buy, sending people away to save for another three
months. Half his church has bought a car from him or asked him about one in the parking lot after
service.

## What he wants from Jubilee

- To be the car person his wider church community can ask before they buy, anywhere.
- A way to be found that does not depend on the dealership's advertising.
- As a father: a tutor for Kezia, and a dentist who is patient with her.

## What worries him

- **He is an employee.** Jubilee is a directory of "Christian-owned businesses". The dealership
  is not his and is not Christian-owned. He will not pretend otherwise.
- **No category fits.** Cars are not in the list of eleven.
- **His trade's reputation.** He expects to be doubted and would rather be checked than waved
  through.
- **Commission.** His income moves month to month. He is careful, and wary of anyone who talks
  about faith and money in the same breath.

## How he serves the mission

Devon is plainly one of the "Christian business people in Toronto" whom the mission says Jubilee
equips, yet he owns no business, and the product as built lists businesses by their owners. He
shows where the mission's words reach further than the directory does today.

## In his words

"I don't own the lot. I own my name. Twelve years of it. Tell me straight whether that's enough
to be on here, and I'll respect the answer either way."
