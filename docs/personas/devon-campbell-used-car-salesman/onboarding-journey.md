# Devon's onboarding journey

[Back to Devon Campbell](README.md)

Devon's journey does not end with a live listing. It is written as it would honestly go today.

## How Devon finds Jubilee

At a men's ministry breakfast, a brother who runs a small moving company passes his phone around
with the elevator pitch on it and says he listed last month. Devon reads "a directory of verified
Christian-owned businesses" and says, to laughter, "So not me, then." The brother forwards him
the pitch on WhatsApp anyway. Devon opens jubilee.to that afternoon between customers.

## Steps

| Step | What Devon does | What Jubilee shows | What Devon thinks or feels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opens the marketing site on his phone | "Toronto’s Christian businesses, in one place.", "Start browsing", "Free for members · Every business verified" | He can use it as a customer at least. He taps "Start browsing" |
| 2 | Looks through the categories on `/welcome` | The eleven categories. None for vehicles | Friction: he searches "used car" and "mechanic" to be sure. Nobody in his trade is there |
| 3 | Searches "math tutor" and opens Bright Path Tutoring | The listing: "Verified Christian business · Learning", services, "Book a meeting" | This he does need. Kezia's report card came last week |
| 4 | Presses "Book a meeting" and joins with e-mail and password | The sign-in prompt, then "Join Jubilee". He fills in the form, home church included, and ticks the agreement | Ordinary. He mistypes the password on the lot and uses the show-password button |
| 5 | Keeps browsing before he confirms | "Confirm your e-mail to message businesses. We sent a link to devon.campbell@example.com." | Fair enough. He confirms at lunch |
| 6 | Books a meeting with the tutor | "Request sent to Bright Path Tutoring." and "How easy was it to send your request?" | He answers 5. The tutor confirms that evening. This is his first valuable moment, as a Member and a father |
| 7 | A week later, opens `/list` on the dealership's desktop | "Free for Christian-owned businesses in Toronto. Every listing is verified before it goes live." | Friction: the first sentence is the problem. Nothing says whether an employee may list |
| 8 | Looks in Help | "How does a business get verified?" and four other questions | No answer to his. He decides to describe himself truthfully and let the team decide |
| 9 | Fills in the form, saves a draft, sleeps on it, submits | "Devon Campbell, Vehicle Sales is in for verification." | Check-in: he answers 2 and writes what got in the way |
| 10 | Two business days later, opens My business | "2 changes needed before we can publish": the description and the category, with the reviewer's note and "Edit listing" | The note is courteous. It asks him to make clearer that he works for the dealership, and says Shops is for businesses selling their own products. It does not say whether he can be listed |
| 11 | Edits the About text. Cannot fix the category | The same eleven categories | Friction: he can meet the first request and not the second. He does not press "Resubmit" |
| 12 | Writes to help@jubilee.example | The Help page promises that "a person answers within one business day" | He asks one question: can a Christian who works in a business he does not own be listed? He is waiting for the answer |

## Where it stands

Devon is a Member with a tutor booked and a listing in "Changes requested". Nothing has been
refused and nothing has been approved.

## Where it could go wrong

- The answer is slow or vague. He will take silence as a no and tell the men's ministry so.
- He is told to pick any category to get through. He would be live under a label that is not
  true, which is the opposite of why he came.
- He is approved without the ownership question being settled, a member assumes the dealership
  is Christian-owned, and feels misled by Jubilee and by him.
- He is declined in a form letter. The reason matters more to him than the outcome.
