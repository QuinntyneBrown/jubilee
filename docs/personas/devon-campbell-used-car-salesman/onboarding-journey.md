# Devon's onboarding journey

[Back to Devon Campbell](README.md)

Devon's journey does not end with a live listing. It is written as it would honestly go today.

## How Devon finds Jubilee

At a men's ministry breakfast, a brother who runs a small moving company passes his phone around
with the elevator pitch on it and says he listed last month. Devon reads "a directory of
Christian-owned businesses" and says, to laughter, "So not me, then." The brother forwards him
the pitch on WhatsApp anyway. Devon opens jubilee.to that afternoon between customers.

## Steps

| Step | What Devon does | What Jubilee shows | What Devon thinks or feels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opens the marketing site on his phone | "Toronto’s Christian businesses, in one place.", "Start browsing", "Free for members · Free to list" | He can use it as a customer at least. He taps "Start browsing" |
| 2 | Looks through the categories on `/welcome` | The eleven categories. None for vehicles | Friction: he searches "used car" and "mechanic" to be sure. Nobody in his trade is there |
| 3 | Searches "math tutor" and opens Bright Path Tutoring | The listing: "Learning", services, "Book a meeting" | This he does need. Kezia's report card came last week |
| 4 | Presses "Book a meeting" and joins with e-mail and password | The sign-in prompt, then "Join Jubilee". He fills in the form, home church included, and ticks the agreement | Ordinary. He mistypes the password on the lot and uses the show-password button |
| 5 | Keeps browsing before he confirms | "Confirm your e-mail to message businesses. We sent a link to devon.campbell@example.com." | Fair enough. He confirms at lunch |
| 6 | Books a meeting with the tutor | "Request sent to Bright Path Tutoring." and "How easy was it to send your request?" | He answers 5. The tutor confirms that evening. This is his first valuable moment, as a Member and a father |
| 7 | A week later, opens `/list` on the dealership's desktop | "Free for Christian-owned businesses in Toronto. Your listing goes live as soon as you publish it." | Friction: the first sentence is the problem. Nothing says whether an employee may list, and nobody will look before it is public. The decision is his |
| 8 | Looks in Help | "How does Jubilee keep listings honest?" and four other questions | No answer to his. He reads that the team takes down listings that misrepresent a business, and wonders whether his would be one |
| 9 | Fills in the form truthfully, picks Shops because nothing fits, and saves a draft | "Draft saved. Saved today at {time}. Come back any time from My business; nothing is public until you submit." | He sleeps on it |
| 10 | The next day, reaches the agreement and "Publish listing" | "I agree to Jubilee's statement of faith and to deal honestly with every customer." | Friction: Shops is not true, and a listing of his would let a member think the dealership is Christian-owned. Dealing honestly means not pressing it on a guess. He does not publish |
| 11 | Writes to help@jubilee.example | The Help page promises that "a person answers within one business day" | He asks one question: can a Christian who works in a business he does not own be listed? He is waiting for the answer |

## Where it stands

Devon is a Member with a tutor booked and a listing saved as a draft. He has not published it,
because nobody has told him whether he may.

## Where it could go wrong

- The answer is slow or vague. He will take silence as a no and tell the men's ministry so.
- He is told to pick any category and publish. He would be live under a label that is not true,
  which is the opposite of why he came.
- He publishes on a guess, a member assumes the dealership is Christian-owned, and feels misled
  by Jubilee and by him.
- His listing is taken down with a one-line reason. The reason matters more to him than the
  outcome.
