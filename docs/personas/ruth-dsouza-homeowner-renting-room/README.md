## Story

Ruth is Goan and came to Toronto from Mumbai in 1981, newly married to Joseph. They bought the
bungalow in 1990 and raised two children in it. Joseph died three years ago. The house is paid
for, but the property tax, the gas and the roof are not small on one pension, and the evenings
are long.

There is a spare bedroom upstairs and a finished basement with its own bathroom. She remembers
what it was to arrive in this city knowing no one, and the parish family who rented her and Joseph
their first room. She would like to do the same for a student or a newcomer, at a fair rent. She
would also like to feel safe in her own home.

## What she wants from Jubilee

- Someone decent in the spare room: company at supper now and then, and help with the bills.
- To offer a fair rent to a student or a newcomer, as was once done for her.
- To be told, step by step, what to do and what is expected of her.

## What worries her

- A stranger in her home, and how she would know anything about them beforehand.
- Her address being seen by people she has not chosen.
- What "Who lives in the home" should say: must she write that she is a widow living alone?
- What she may and may not ask a renter, and whether she may say she would prefer a woman.

## How she serves the mission

Ruth has no business. She has a house, and she means to use it for someone else's good, as in the
passage the mission's "resource His Kingdom" rests on, where those who followed Jesus "served
them from their possessions." (Luke 8:3) A room at a fair rent is what she has to give.

## In her words

"I don't need a tenant. I need a good person in the house who will also pay something."
