# Victoria's onboarding journey

[Back to Victoria Lam](README.md)

## How Victoria finds Jubilee

A fellow member of the finance committee, who runs a print shop, sends her the elevator pitch on
WhatsApp one Sunday afternoon. She reads it twice. "God gave you the ability to build. He also
gave it a purpose." lands. She does not think of herself as running a business, so she nearly
sets it aside, until she opens the app and sees "Rooms & rentals" second among the categories.

## Steps

| Step | What Victoria does | What Jubilee shows | What Victoria thinks or feels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opens `/welcome` on her phone in the car outside a hockey rink | "Do business with Toronto's Christian community.", "Join free", "Browse businesses", the eleven categories | Looks for who is behind it and how listings are checked before anything else |
| 2 | Opens Rooms & rentals and reads to the end of the page | "Have a spare room, suite or hall?", "Rent it to someone from the community. Free to list.", "List a space" | Friction: "someone from the community" is what she wants and what worries her. Is she being asked to choose tenants by faith? |
| 3 | Presses "List a space", is sent to join, uses "Continue with Google" | `/join`, then the checkbox "I am 18 or older and agree to Jubilee’s statement of faith and terms." | Reads the statement of faith and the terms in full. Ticks |
| 4 | Arrives on `/list?kind=space` | "List a space", "How are you renting it?" with four terms, "Long term" chosen | Clear. She starts with the downtown one-bedroom |
| 5 | Fills in the space fields (see [profile setup](profile-setup-journey.md)) | "Exact address" with "Only the renter whose request you accept sees it." | Reassured. The address of a tenanted building is not something she publishes |
| 6 | Reaches the agreement | "I agree to Jubilee’s statement of faith, and confirm I’m allowed to rent this space and will follow Ontario’s tenancy rules." | She is glad the line is there. She notes that it does not mention the Human Rights Code |
| 7 | Presses "Submit for verification" | "1-bedroom condo near St. Lawrence Market is in for verification. Nothing more to do right now. We'll e-mail you the moment you're live." | Friction: she wants Joanne to add the photos. There is one owner account per listing, and she will not share her password |
| 8 | Adds six photos herself at 11 p.m. from My business | "Up to 6 photos, JPG or PNG, 5 MB each. The first is your cover." | Done, grudgingly |
| 9 | Starts a second space for the duplex's upper unit | The switcher at the top of My business shows both | Counts: five homes, and a limit of 5 listings. No room to spare |
| 10 | Is live the next afternoon; within a week has three "Request a viewing" requests for the condo | Incoming requests: "Move in Mon 1 Feb · 12 months", "Decline" and "Confirm" | First valuable moment, and a new problem: three requests, each a name, a date and a note. Nothing she can fairly compare |

## Where it could go wrong

- She confirms the first request because she recognises the surname from church. That is the
  favouritism she set out to avoid, and the product gave her nothing else to decide on.
- "Confirm" releases the exact address. For a viewing that is right, but to her "Confirm request"
  reads like accepting a tenant, and she hesitates over every one.
- A sixth listing is refused: "You can list up to 5 businesses. Write to help@jubilee.example for
  more." Calling her homes "businesses" is the part she would mind.
- Someone at church notices five listings under "Victoria" and mentions it at coffee hour.
