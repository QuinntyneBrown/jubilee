## What she hesitates over

- **The price of the duplex.** The market rent is about $3,400. She wants to offer it at $2,600 to
  a family who needs it. If she types 2600, everyone sees a low rent and the unit goes to whoever
  asks first, not to the family she has in mind. If she types 3400, that family never asks. She
  types 3400, dislikes it, and saves the draft without publishing.
- **Short stay for the furnished condo.** A space has exactly one term. She would need a second
  listing for the same unit, her building restricts short rentals, and she has no listings to
  spare. She drops the idea.
- **Her name.** The host card shows "Your host" and the owner's name. Five times.
- **House rules.** She writes only what a lease may lawfully require. She leaves "Pets OK" unset
  and adds no rule on pets, because she knows a lease cannot forbid them in most rentals.

## What the profile cannot say

- That one unit is held at a rent below the market for a household in need, shown only to those
  for whom it is meant.
- That Joanne answers messages and shows the units: no delegate account, no second name.
- What she will ask of every applicant, the same for all: income or a guarantor, references, and
  a signed standard lease.
- That the five spaces are one household's stewardship and not a property company, without
  listing them side by side.
