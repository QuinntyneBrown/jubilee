## Story

Victoria was born in Hong Kong and came to Toronto at 12. She bought her first condo at 29 with
savings and a loan from her parents, and added the others over fifteen years. She does not talk
about them at church. She calls the properties a stewardship: homes she is answerable for, to her
tenants and to God, more than a portfolio.

Two units come vacant this winter: a one-bedroom downtown and the upper unit of the East York
duplex, three bedrooms. She would like them to go to people from the community, and she would
like to do it rightly. She knows from her day job how selection rules work, and she will not put
her name to a process that picks tenants by who knows whom.

## What she wants from Jubilee

- Tenants she can trust and who can trust her, found through a community and not an anonymous
  classified site.
- A way to hold the duplex's upper unit at a lower rent for a family that needs it, quietly.
- To hand the day-to-day (photos, messages, viewings) to Joanne.

## What worries her

- Favouritism: renting to a friend of a friend and calling it fellowship.
- Her wealth on display: five listings under one name, visible to her own congregation.
- Ontario's tenancy and human-rights rules on choosing tenants, and whether "Rent it to someone
  from the community." invites her to break them.

## How she serves the mission

Victoria has prospered. The mission's phrase for her is "use what they earn to support God's work": what she asks of
Jubilee is help in making five homes serve people, without being flattered for it.

## In her words

"I don't want a thank-you from the pulpit. I want a good family in that unit and a clean file."
