## Story

Sofia came to Toronto from Manila at eleven. She worked in a portrait studio in a mall for eight
years, then started on her own when her first child was born, with a camera, a spare room and the
families of her parish. Baptisms led to first communions, first communions to family sessions,
and family sessions to weddings.

Her week is built around two school runs and Paolo's shifts. She shoots on weekends and in the
late afternoon light, edits at night, and answers clients from the school yard. From September
to December she is booked most Saturdays, and every unanswered message is a session she may lose.

## What she wants from Jubilee

- To show her work. People hire a photographer with their eyes.
- Bookings she can confirm from her phone in a minute, on dates that are truly free.
- A deposit that holds the date. A booking without one is a hope, not a booking.
- Families from beyond her own parish.

## What worries her

- Six photos. Her last wedding gallery had 640.
- Double-booking, because her real calendar lives somewhere else.
- Looking slow. If she is at a ceremony all Saturday, she cannot reply until evening.
- Charging the people she worships with. She gives her parish a lower price and does not want
  that to become what everyone expects.

## How she serves the mission

Sofia's work is the faithful, careful kind the mission means by "do well in business": the
studio pays for her children's school and lets her photograph every baptism in her parish for a
price any family can manage. Much of what she records is the life of the church itself.

## In her words

"Give me twenty photos and a calendar and I will never leave. Six photos is a business card."
