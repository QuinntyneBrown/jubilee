## Story

Joshua is Malaysian Chinese and came to Toronto at 18. He and a classmate took a two-bedroom at
$2,900 a month in second year. In October the classmate left for a co-op placement in Ottawa, and
since then Joshua has paid the whole rent from savings, shifts and one hard telephone call to his
parents. He can do that for about two more weeks.

He wants a roommate of about his age who keeps similar hours, does not mind a guitar and will not
mind that he prays before meals. In May he goes home for four months and would like someone to
take his room while he is away, so that he is not paying rent on an empty bed.

## What he wants from Jubilee

- A roommate within two weeks.
- Someone he can live with: a student or a recent graduate, tidy enough, with quiet hours near
  his own.
- To sublet his room from May to August without it becoming a second project.

## What worries him

- He rents. He needs his landlord's consent to take a roommate or to sublet, and he has not asked.
- "Christian-owned business" does not sound like a student with a spare bedroom.
- No photos until after he publishes: his room is live before it has a picture, and on his phone a
  listing without photos is not a listing.

## How he serves the mission

Joshua is not yet one of the "Christian business people in Toronto" the mission names, though an
engineer of 21 may be one in ten years. What he shows today is the mission's first phrase,
"Abiding in Jesus and for love of Him", in a small matter: telling the truth on a form when a
quicker answer was one tap away.

## In his words

"I can't tick a box that says I'm allowed if I haven't asked. Give me a minute. Okay, a day."
