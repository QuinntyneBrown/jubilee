## Story

Eleanor taught art to children for thirty-one years and retired the spring before her husband,
Peter, died. Lettering was what she did in the evenings that first winter. A friend on the altar
guild asked for a set of cards, then the parish asked for a table at the bazaar, and now she makes
greeting cards, Scripture cards, notepads and, two or three times a year, a set of wedding
invitations.

She sells at church bazaars and craft fairs, for cash, from a folding table. She does not think of
herself as a business person. She would like her cards to reach beyond the parishes that already
know her, and she would like not to carry the table any more.

## What she wants from Jubilee

- A simple place where people can see her cards and ask for them.
- To understand exactly what happens when someone sends an order request, since no money changes
  hands on Jubilee.
- To post cards to buyers, take custom orders, and offer Christmas and Easter cards in season.

## What worries her

- Putting her home address on the internet. She lives alone.
- Doing something wrong: sending cards and not being paid, or missing a request for a week.
- That "business" means tax forms and obligations she does not understand.

## How she serves the mission

Eleanor's work is small and done with care, and the mission's "do well in business" does not ask
for more than that: faithfulness with what was entrusted. Much of what she earns at the bazaar goes
back to the parish that hosts it.

## In her words

"I am not trying to build an empire. I should just like the cards to go to people who will send
them."
