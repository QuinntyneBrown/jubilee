# Eleanor Whitfield: onboarding journey

Back to [Eleanor Whitfield](README.md).

## How Eleanor finds Jubilee

At her parish's Christmas bazaar in mid-November, a young woman buys four sets of cards and asks
whether Eleanor sells online. Eleanor says no. The woman writes "jubilee.to" on the back of one of
Eleanor's own price tags and says, "It's for Christian-owned businesses. It's free, and they don't
take a cut." Eleanor keeps the tag on the fridge for nine days. Then her daughter Claire, on the
Sunday telephone call from Ottawa, says, "Mum, open it now, while I'm on the line."

## Steps

| Step | What Eleanor does | What Jubilee shows | What Eleanor thinks or feels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Types the address into her iPad. | The marketing site: "Toronto’s Christian businesses, in one place." and "Watch the film". | She watches the film twice. It is gentler than she feared. |
| 2 | Scrolls to "Run a Christian-owned business? Be found." and presses "List your business free". | `/join`: "Join Jubilee", "Free for everyone in Toronto’s Christian community." | "Am I a business?" Claire says yes. |
| 3 | Fills in the form slowly. Ignores the Google button; she does not know whether she has a Google account. | First name, Last name, Email, Password ("At least 10 characters."), Home church (optional). | Friction: the password. She writes it in her address book, then uses the show-password button to compare. |
| 4 | Reads the statement of faith in full before ticking the box. | The statement of faith, opened from the checkbox line; her typed answers are still there when she returns. | She was afraid of losing the form. She did not. She agrees with what she read. |
| 5 | Sees "Check your e-mail" and does not know where her e-mail is. | The confirmation link, which works once, for 24 hours. | Friction: Claire talks her to the Mail icon. Without the call she would have stopped here. |
| 6 | Presses "List your business" after confirming. | "E-mail confirmed", then `/list`: "What do you offer?" | She chooses "Products" at once. That one is clear. |
| 7 | Reaches "Store address" and stops. | The field for a shop's address. | Friction, and the worst of it. She has no store. She does not want her house shown. She presses "Save draft" and rings off. |
| 8 | Comes back on Tuesday, having written to the help address and been answered. Finishes the form, adds three products, submits. | "Draft saved." with her values restored; then "We review your listing. Within two business days". | The draft being there, exactly as she left it, earns her trust more than anything else. |
| 9 | Answers "How easy was it to list your business?" | Scores 1 to 5; a low score asks "What got in the way?" | Scores it 3 and writes: "I work from home and was not sure about the address." |
| 10 | Receives the e-mail that she is live. | "Whitfield Cards & Lettering is verified and live. Members can find you in Shops." | She telephones Claire. |
| 11 | Gets her first order request: two sets of greeting cards, to be posted to Etobicoke. Confirms it. | The request, then "Order confirmed. {Customer} has been told. Message him about payment and pickup." | The first valuable moment, and a puzzle: now what? She writes a careful message asking for an e-transfer and a postal address. |

## Where it could go wrong

- No daughter on the telephone at step 5, and the confirmation link expires unopened.
- "Store address" has no "I work from home" choice, and she abandons the draft for good.
- She confirms an order, posts the cards and is not sure how to ask for payment, or is not paid.
- A request arrives while she is in Ottawa with her grandchild; she has not set an away period
  and the member waits a week.
