## Story

Marcus spent fourteen years at a bank, the last six as a branch financial planner with sales
targets. He left when he could no longer recommend the product of the month with a clear
conscience, and set up on his own. Nine years on he looks after about 180 families, most of them
found through one family telling another.

He is licensed and regulated, and everything he publishes about his practice is read by a
compliance officer first. He is also a deacon, and he has a rule he has never broken: he does not
raise business at church. If someone from church wants his help, they ask him.

## What he wants from Jubilee

- A place where families can find him without his having to approach them.
- His licences and designations shown plainly, because in his profession they are the first thing
  an honest client should check.
- A record of what was said and asked, which his profession requires him to keep.

## What worries him

- **Compliance.** A listing is advertising. Every word of it needs approval, and a rule he breaks
  by accident is still broken.
- **Being seen to sell to his church.** A deacon with a listing could look like a deacon with a
  motive.
- **Money and Scripture side by side.** He has sat through seminars that promised wealth to the
  faithful. He read the pitch twice to be sure Jubilee was not another one.

## How he serves the mission

Marcus helps families do well and "support God's work": he asks every new client what the money is for
before he asks how much there is. He holds his own work to one verse, which he keeps to himself
and does not print on anything: "He who is faithful in a very little is faithful also in much. He
who is dishonest in a very little is also dishonest in much." (Luke 16:10)

## In his words

"I can't tell anyone their money will grow. I can tell them what it costs, what it covers and
what I am paid. If Jubilee lets me say that clearly, I'm in."
