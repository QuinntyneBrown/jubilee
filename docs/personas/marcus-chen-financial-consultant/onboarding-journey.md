# Marcus's onboarding journey

[Back to Marcus Chen](README.md)

## How Marcus finds Jubilee

A fellow deacon who runs a printing business forwards him the one-page elevator pitch on WhatsApp
on a Tuesday evening, with the message that goes with it. Marcus reads it at his desk after the
children are in bed. He checks the verses it quotes against his own Bible, notes that the
page asks him to list a business and promises him nothing in return, and decides to look further.

## Steps

| Step | What Marcus does | What Jubilee shows | What Marcus thinks or feels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reads the pitch on his phone | "Your business was meant to outlast you." and the mission, word for word | Wary. He is looking for a promise of prosperity and does not find one |
| 2 | Opens jubilee.to on his laptop the next morning | "Toronto’s Christian businesses, in one place.", "Start browsing", "Watch the film", "Free for members · Every business verified" | He watches the 1:04 film with the sound on. Calm, no testimonies of sudden wealth |
| 3 | Scrolls to "Run a Christian-owned business? Be found." | "List your business free" | He does not press it yet. He wants the terms first |
| 4 | Opens the app and reads the statement of faith, the terms and the privacy page | In the terms: "Jubilee does not take payment or hold funds; you pay the business directly." | Relief. Nothing here touches client money, which keeps Jubilee outside the hardest of his rules |
| 5 | Presses "List your business free" and joins with e-mail and password | "Join Jubilee". He skips the Google button and fills in the form, home church included ("Shown to no one. Helps us recommend businesses near you.") | He reads the hint twice. "Shown to no one" matters to a deacon who does not want his church attached to his listing |
| 6 | Ticks "I am 18 or older and agree to Jubilee’s statement of faith and terms." | "Check your e-mail" | He has already read both, so he ticks it without unease |
| 7 | Confirms his e-mail | "E-mail confirmed", with "Start browsing" and "List your business" | Straightforward |
| 8 | Fills in `/list` and presses "Save draft" | "Draft saved. Saved today at 10:42 a.m. Come back any time from My business; nothing is public until you submit." | Exactly what he needed: he cannot submit until compliance has read it |
| 9 | Copies the wording into an e-mail to his compliance officer | Nothing. Jubilee has no way to share a draft with a third person | Friction: he retypes the listing into a document. The answer takes four days |
| 10 | Submits for verification | "Chen Family Financial is in for verification. Nothing more to do right now. We'll e-mail you the moment you're live." and "We review your listing. Within two business days" | He answers the check-in with a 4 |
| 11 | Opens My business the next afternoon | "Chen Family Financial is verified and live. Members can find you in Financial & insurance." | Pleased, and a little uneasy: nobody asked for his licence number |
| 12 | Nine days later, confirms a booking from a couple expecting their first child | The request under "Needs your reply", with "Decline" and "Confirm" | The first valuable moment. They found him; he did not approach them |

## Where it could go wrong

- If the terms had been vague about payment, he would have stopped at step 4.
- If compliance had refused the wording, there is no partial listing to fall back on: the About
  text and the services are where everything he must say has to live.
- If a member of his own church books him and later feels sold to, the cost is to his service as
  a deacon, not to his practice.
- An unlicensed person could list in Financial & insurance with the same "Verified" mark he
  carries. If one does harm, members will not tell the two apart.
