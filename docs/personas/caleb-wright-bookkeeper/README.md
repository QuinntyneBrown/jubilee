## Story

Caleb's family has been in Nova Scotia for more than two hundred years. He came to Toronto in 1989
for work, a few years after a retinal condition took his sight in his twenties. He retrained in
accounting software as it became usable with a screen reader, kept the books of a printing firm for
eleven years, and went out on his own in 2004. Today he has nineteen clients: contractors, a
bakery, a daycare and two churches.

He has no patience for being admired. He wants to be hired because the books balance. He has
abandoned many websites at the sign-up form, and he judges a product in its first two minutes: are
the fields labelled, does focus go where it should, does the page tell him what just happened.

## What he wants from Jubilee

- Clients who value an honest set of books: small businesses and churches near East York.
- Trades he can hire without having to inspect the finished work by eye: a roofer and an
  electrician he can trust at their word.
- A listing form and an owner workspace he can run entirely by himself, without Denise reading
  the screen to him.

## What worries him

- A picture CAPTCHA at joining, an unlabelled button, a date picker that traps the keyboard.
- Photos: his listing needs them, he cannot see them, and other owners' photos may say nothing to
  him if their alt text is left at the default.
- That the pitch film's captions are part of the picture, so the words on screen exist only as
  pixels.

## How he serves the mission

Caleb is one of the "Christian business owners in Toronto" the mission names, and his trade is
faithfulness with what others entrust to him. If Jubilee "helps" him, it has to help him without
sight, or the word does not hold.

## In his words

"Don't tell me the site is accessible. Let me tab through it and I'll tell you."
