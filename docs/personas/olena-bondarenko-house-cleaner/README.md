## Story

Olena kept the books for a building-supplies company in Kharkiv for fourteen years. She arrived in
Toronto with her son in 2022. Her qualifications did not transfer, her English was a school
subject from long ago, and cleaning houses was work she could start the same week. Within a year
she had more houses than hours, brought in Iryna, and began to run it properly: a schedule, a
price list, a ledger that balances.

English is her third language, after Ukrainian and Russian. She reads it with her browser's
translation and writes it carefully, then asks Maksym to check. She has no Canadian credit history
and few people here who could be called a reference. What she has is four families who have given
her their keys.

## What she wants from Jubilee

- Steady weekly and biweekly clients, close enough to reach by transit.
- To be judged by her work, and treated fairly: paid on the day, not bargained down.
- To know something about a household before she and Iryna walk into it.
- One day, a second listing for bookkeeping.

## What worries her

- Misunderstanding a message, or being misunderstood, and losing a client over a word.
- Going alone into a stranger's home on the strength of a first name.
- Being asked for references, a credit check or papers she cannot yet produce.

## How she serves the mission

The mission speaks of "Christian business people in Toronto" without asking how long they have
been here or what they did before. Olena runs a two-person business with an accountant's honesty,
and the parish that helped her settle is the first place her giving goes.

## In her words

"In Kharkiv I counted other people's money and it was always correct. Here I clean, and it is also
always correct. The work changed. I did not."
