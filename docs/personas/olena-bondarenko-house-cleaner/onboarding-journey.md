# Olena Bondarenko: onboarding journey

Back to [Olena Bondarenko](README.md).

## How Olena finds Jubilee

One of Olena's clients, a retired teacher whose house she has cleaned every Thursday for two
years, leaves a note on the kitchen counter with the week's envelope: "Olena, you should be on
this. I would vouch for you to anyone." Under it is a printed page: the Jubilee pitch. Olena
photographs it, translates it on the streetcar home, and reads the mission twice, once in each
language, to be sure the translation has not softened it.

## Steps

| Step | What Olena does | What Jubilee shows | What Olena thinks or feels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opens jubilee.to on her phone; the browser offers to translate and she accepts. | The marketing site, in machine-translated Ukrainian: "Toronto’s Christian businesses, in one place." | Friction from the first screen: the app is English only. The translation is readable, but she switches back to English for anything she must agree to. |
| 2 | Presses "Start browsing" and opens "Home & property". | Businesses, with filter chips: "Free quote", "Emergency calls", "Open Saturdays", and the areas served. | She counts the cleaners. Few. She reads their listings closely, as she once read a competitor's price list. |
| 3 | Looks for what Jubilee will ask of her. Opens "List your business" from the "Own a business?" panel. | The sign-in prompt, then `/join`. | She expects the usual: references, a credit check, a licence. She braces. |
| 4 | Joins with e-mail and a password. Leaves "Home church address" empty and types her parish's name. | "Free for everyone in Toronto’s Christian community." and "Shown to no one. Helps us recommend businesses near you." | She reads the statement of faith in English with a dictionary open. It takes twenty minutes. She agrees and means it. |
| 5 | Confirms her e-mail within the hour. | "E-mail confirmed" with "List your business". | Simple. |
| 6 | Opens `/list` and reads the whole form before typing a word. | "Free for Christian-owned businesses in Toronto. Your listing goes live as soon as you publish it." No reference, no credit question, no licence field. | Relief, and then suspicion: "Nobody checks, then? So anyone can say anything." She reads in Help that the Jubilee team takes down listings that misrepresent a business. |
| 7 | Chooses "Services" and "Home & property"; writes each line in a translator, then asks Maksym to read it. | "How members see you", marked "Not published yet". "Save draft". | Friction: the translated page shows the preview of her English words in Ukrainian, so she turns translation off to see what members will see. |
| 8 | Presses "Publish listing" on the third evening. | "Olena's Home Cleaning is live. Members can find you in Home & property now." "How easy was it to list your business?" | Scores it 4. The form was easy. The English was not. She shows Maksym, then Iryna, then sends a photograph of the screen to her mother in Poltava. |
| 9 | Sees her card is a tinted tile beside cleaners with photos, and adds photos from "Edit listing". | "Up to 6 photos, JPG or PNG, 5 MB each. The first is your cover." | Friction: her work is inside other people's homes, and she will not photograph those. She photographs her kit, and her own kitchen after cleaning it. |
| 10 | Receives a booking for a free first visit in Etobicoke and confirms it, after messaging to ask who will be home. | The request, with the member's first name and note; the conversation. | The first valuable moment, with a caution in it. The member answers warmly. Olena takes Iryna with her anyway. |

## Where it could go wrong

- A translated button or error message says something slightly different from the English, and
  she presses the wrong one or agrees to something she did not intend.
- A member writes in quick, idiomatic English; she misreads it, arrives on the wrong day, and
  loses the client.
- Unsure what photo of her work she may honestly show, she leaves the tinted tile, and members
  pass over her listing for the cleaners with photos.
- Her first visit to a stranger's home goes badly, and nothing in Jubilee told her who the member
  was beyond a first name.
