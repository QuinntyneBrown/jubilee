## Story

Luis was born in Medellín and came to Toronto at 22. He washed cars, then apprenticed for five
years at a dealership, earned his licence and, six years ago, took over the lease on a two-bay
garage in Weston when its owner retired. He kept the old customers by doing one thing the same way
every time: he shows people the worn part before he replaces it.

He trains one apprentice, a young man from his church. Most of his customers come by word of
mouth, in Spanish and in English. He works with his hands all day, so the phone is answered late:
he reads messages after the children are asleep, usually past ten at night.

## What he wants from Jubilee

- To be found by people who need a mechanic they can trust, including in an emergency.
- Quote requests that come with the year, make, model and a photo, so he can answer once.
- His opening hours shown plainly, Saturday morning included.
- A way to say he is licensed.

## What worries him

- There is no automotive category. He will be filed somewhere he does not belong.
- A member in trouble on the roadside will message him at noon and he will see it at eleven at
  night.
- That "Christian mechanic" could be read as a promise of cheap work. He charges fair, not low.

## How he serves the mission

Luis is one of the "Christian business people in Toronto" the mission names: a small trade run
faithfully, where honest measures are the whole reputation. Training an apprentice from his church
is how he understands "prosper with purpose".

## In his words

"I don't need people to find the cheapest garage. I need them to find one that tells the truth
about their brakes."
