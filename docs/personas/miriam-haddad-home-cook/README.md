## Story

Miriam left Lebanon in 1994, newly married, and has cooked for other people's occasions ever
since: first as a favour, then because people pressed money into her hand, and for the last
fifteen years as steady work that helped put three children through school. Nobody has ever found
her by an advertisement. They taste her kibbeh at a funeral lunch and ask whose it is.

Orders come by telephone and by WhatsApp, often as a voice note: how many people, which day. She
keeps them in a spiral notebook by the stove. She needs three days' notice, a minimum size of
order, and to be told about allergies, and she repeats all three in every call.

## What she wants from Jubilee

- Orders from beyond her own parish, without giving up the telephone.
- A page that says, once, what she makes, how much notice she needs and what is in the food.
- For Rita to be able to help her answer.

## What worries her

- The rules for selling food made at home. She has heard there are some and has never been sure
  which apply to her, and she fears that listing will make her answerable for them.
- That no category is for food.
- That writing it all down makes it a "business", with all that follows.

## How she serves the mission

Miriam has fed her parish at its funerals and its feasts for thirty years, often at cost. She is
already doing what the mission calls "support God's work"; Jubilee's part is to equip her to be
found and to be paid fairly for the rest.

## In her words

"When someone dies, the family should not have to think about lunch. That is my work. The trays
for birthdays, those pay for the flour."
