# Daniel's onboarding journey

[Back to Daniel Okafor](README.md)

## How Daniel finds Jubilee

On a Thursday night after rehearsal, Daniel asks the sound team's WhatsApp group whether anyone
knows an accountant who handles freelance income. The drummer answers with a shared listing link
(L2-038) for Harbourlight Accounting and one line: "She did ours. She's on Jubilee." Daniel opens
the link on his phone on the streetcar home.

## Steps

| Step | What Daniel does | What Jubilee shows | What Daniel thinks or feels |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opens the shared link as a Visitor | The listing page: "Financial & insurance", the services with prices, "Book a meeting" and "Message" | "No sign-up wall. Good." He reads the services before anything else |
| 2 | Opens Help from the footer | The Help answer to "How does Jubilee keep listings honest?" | He wanted to know who stands behind a listing. Owners agree to deal honestly and the Jubilee team takes down listings that misrepresent a business. Plain, shorter than he hoped, and it does not pretend to more |
| 3 | Presses "Book a meeting" | The sign-in prompt, with "Sign in" and "Join free" | Expected. He presses "Join free" |
| 4 | Chooses "Sign up with Google" on `/join` | "Join Jubilee", "Free for everyone in Toronto’s Christian community.", the Google button first, then "or use email" | One tap. No password to invent, no e-mail to confirm |
| 5 | Reads and accepts the agreement | The statement of faith and terms agreement, shown before the account can be used | He opens the statement of faith and reads all of it. He agrees with it, and is glad it was not hidden behind a pre-ticked box |
| 6 | Lands back on the listing | The same listing, with "Book a meeting with Harbourlight Accounting" already open: "Ask for a time. Ngozi confirms it or suggests another; nothing is charged." | "It remembered what I was doing." This is the moment he decides the product is well made |
| 7 | Picks the service, a day, "Video call", a time, and writes two lines about his freelance income | "Request sent to Harbourlight Accounting. Ngozi usually confirms within a day. You'll get a notification and an e-mail, and the request is in Your requests." | Clear. He knows what happens next and where to look |
| 8 | Answers the check-in | "How easy was it to send your request?" He picks 5: "Thanks, that helps us." | A single question he does not mind |
| 9 | Next morning, opens the notification | "Ngozi confirmed your meeting", with "Add to calendar" | Friction: "Add to calendar" downloads an `.ics` file. He wanted the meeting to appear in Google Calendar by itself |
| 10 | Two weeks later, searches "movers" and asks Steadfast Movers for a quote | "Ask Steadfast Movers for a quote", with "What do you need?", "Needed by", "Budget" and "Phone" | He fills in the phone here and notices the hint that it is shared for this request only |

The first valuable moment is step 7: a meeting asked for with a person someone he trusts has
already used, four minutes after opening a link.

## Where it could go wrong

- If Google sign-in were not offered, Daniel would have closed the tab at step 4 and e-mailed the
  accountant from her website. He does not create passwords for things he has not yet used.
- If the booking dialog had not reopened at step 6, he would have assumed the request was lost.
- If the Help answer on how listings stay honest were vague, he would have treated every listing as an
  advert.
- If the accountant had taken three days to answer, the church group chat would have heard.
