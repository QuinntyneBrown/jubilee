## Story

Daniel was born in Toronto to parents who came from Nigeria in the 1980s. He studied computer
science, spent his twenties at agencies, and now works on the dispatch software of a logistics
start-up. Two or three evenings a week he builds websites for small businesses, mostly people who
found him through church or through Chidinma's colleagues.

He and Chidinma are saving for a house and plan to move out of the condo next year. That is what
brought him to Jubilee: he needed an accountant who understands a salary plus self-employed
income, and a mover he could trust with a third-floor walk-down. The idea of listing his own
freelance work came later.

## What he wants from Jubilee

- People he can hire without a week of research: an accountant now, a mover in the spring.
- Sign-in that takes one tap, and requests that land in the calendar he already uses.
- For his freelance work: a few good clients a year, not a flood. He has six free hours a week.
- A product that is built well. He will forgive a missing feature sooner than a careless one.

## What worries him

- That a listing means less than it looks like. Nobody checks a business before it goes live, so he
  wants to know how a dishonest one is caught.
- That a listing commits him to answering requests during his day job.
- That his evenings fill up and the sound desk, and Chidinma, get what is left.

## How he serves the mission

Daniel is one of the "Christian business people in Toronto" the mission names, though his
business is six hours a week. He wants the side work to "prosper with purpose": it pays for the
down payment, and each year he builds one church or charity website without charge.

## In his words

"I'll join anything that lets me sign in with Google and doesn't waste my time. I'll stay if the
people on it turn out to be as honest as the listing says."
