Content and tone

Jubilee speaks like a trusted member of the congregation who also runs a good business: warm, plain and direct. It never preaches and never sells.

Overview

The words in the mocks are design decisions as much as the colours. This page collects them; the Content foundation holds the full microcopy library. Copy is Canadian English ("e-mail", "neighbour", "cheque"), sentence case everywhere, contractions welcome. The Log in and Sign up screens take the design's words, "Log in" and "Email"; the rest of the product keeps "Sign in" and "E-mail" until one change of vocabulary is made across it (ADR 0038).

States

The same object is worded differently through its lifecycle. Keep these words fixed so badges, toasts, notifications and e-mail agree.

Responsive behaviour

  • Write for 360px first: a button label that wraps on a phone is too long. Three words is the target ("Send request", "Find another plumber").
  • Page titles may wrap to two lines; they never truncate. Taglines are capped at 90 characters so they fit two lines on a card.
  • Truncation (.truncate) is only for snippets with the full text one tap away (message previews, thread names).
  • Do not hide meaning in responsive variants: the phone tab bar says "List" for "List your business" only because the icon and the page title carry the rest.

Accessibility

Pattern and roles

Content is the accessibility layer people hear. Accessible names, live-region text and alt text follow the same voice as visible copy. Link text follows WCAG 2.4.4 (purpose from the link text or its sentence); headings follow 2.4.6. See the APG guidance on names and descriptions.

Keyboard

KeyAction
Screen-reader heading listEvery page’s headings read as an outline: "Your requests", then section titles. No "Section 1".

Focus

Focus lands on headings and messages that make sense alone ("Fix 2 things to continue"), never on "Error".

Labelling and announcements

Accessible names add the object visible context provides: "Delete Gold cross stoneware mug", "Account menu for Joanna Adeyemi", "Notifications, 3 unread". Hidden prefixes like "Unread." and "You:" in chat end with punctuation so they are read as separate phrases. Abbreviations are expanded once ("RRSP" in context is fine; "M4C" is a postal code and stays).

Motion

Copy never relies on animation ("look below", "watch the highlight").

Touch

Never write "click" or "tap": use "select", "choose" or the action itself ("Tap the heart" is the one accepted exception, matching the heart icon on phones).
