Pattern

Feedback and loading

Feedback answers two questions a member always has: is it working, and did it work? Jubilee answers both as close to the action as possible, at the smallest size that will be noticed.

Overview

Jubilee has six kinds of feedback. They are chosen by where the action happened and how long the wait is, never by taste: busy button (aria-busy), Skeleton, Spinner, inline status (.inline-status), Alert and banner and Toast. A finished task that changes what the member does next gets a full outcome view built from the Empty state layout.

  • Feedback lives where the eyes are. A button that sends shows it is sending; a switch that saves shows "Saved" beside its section; a page that loads shows its own shape.
  • Every visual change has a text twin in a live region, so the same feedback reaches screen-reader users.
  • Success is quiet, failure is loud and stays. Success toasts auto-dismiss; errors never do and are announced assertively.
  • Say what happens next. "Trevor usually confirms within a day. You’ll get a notification and an e-mail."

Responsive behaviour

  • Skeletons use the same grid as the content they stand for (two-column card grid on phones, four on desktop), so nothing jumps when data arrives.
  • Toasts rise from the bottom; on phones they sit above the tab bar (body:has(.tabbar) .toast-region) and span the width minus the 16px margin.
  • Busy buttons keep their width at every size, so a block button on a phone does not shrink to a spinner.
  • Inline status wraps under the section title on narrow cards rather than truncating.

Accessibility

Pattern and roles

Status messages use role="status" (polite) and errors role="alert" (assertive), the APG alert pattern; this meets WCAG 4.1.3 Status Messages. Busy controls set aria-busy="true". Skeletons are aria-hidden="true" because they carry no information.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabA busy button stays focusable so focus is not lost; activating it again does nothing.
EscDoes not cancel a sending request. Long operations offer a separate Cancel.
Tab into a toastToast actions (Undo, Try again) are reachable; the auto-dismiss timer pauses while focus or the pointer is inside.

Focus

Feedback never moves focus by itself, with two exceptions: a failed submit moves focus to the error summary, and a success view moves focus to its heading or primary. Toasts and inline status never take focus.

Labelling and announcements

Each live region exists in the page before its text changes (an empty .toast-region is in every mock) so screen readers pick up the change. Text says the result, not the mechanism: "Saved at 9:33 a.m.", not "Success".

Motion

Skeleton shimmer and the pulse dot stop under prefers-reduced-motion (animation: none); the spinner keeps turning because it is the only sign of progress. Toast enter and leave use --duration-base, zeroed under reduced motion.

Touch

Toast actions and the close button are 24px minimum (--target-min) with 8px between them; nothing in a toast needs a precise tap.

Content

  • Loading text is the thing plus an ellipsis: "Loading your requests…", "Checking your link…". Never "Please wait".
  • Busy buttons keep their label ("Send request"); the progress text goes into the live region.
  • Success says what changed and what happens next, in the past tense: "Request cancelled. WaterWorks Plumbing & Drains has been told."
  • Saved times are precise: "Saved at 9:33 a.m.", "Saved today at 9:36 a.m."
  • Failures follow the error formula in Content and tone: what happened, that nothing is lost, what to do.
