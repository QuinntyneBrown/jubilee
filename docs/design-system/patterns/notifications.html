Pattern

Notifications

Jubilee’s messages run on a request lifecycle: a member asks, a business confirms or declines, Jubilee verifies listings. Each change reaches the right person once, in the channel that fits how urgent and how lasting it is.

Overview

Five channels, from most fleeting to most lasting: the Toast (a result or a live event, gone in 8 seconds), the banner (.alert.banner, a condition that holds until it changes: offline, e-mail not confirmed, listing pending or paused), the inline Alert and banner (a fact about this page: a decline reason, a draft saved), the notification centre (the record of every event) and the count Badge on the bell, Messages and Requests. E-mail mirrors the centre and is set in Notification settings.

When to use

  • Toast when the event is timely but not lasting, and the member is already doing something else: "New message from Legacy Financial", "Request cancelled · Undo".
  • Banner when a condition limits what the member can do across pages and lasts until something changes. It sits under the top bar, full width, and is dismissible only when the condition is informational (back online, verified and live).
  • Inline alert when the fact belongs to the content of one page: the decline reason on the request, "Waiting for your church reference" on My business.
  • Notification centre for every event a person might want to find again. Toasts and e-mail point here; nothing appears only in a toast.
  • Never a dialog for a notification. Dialogs are for decisions.

Structure

A connection banner under the top bar and a stack of three toasts (success with Undo, a live message, an error that stays). The region is a polite live region; the error toast adds role="alert".

Responsive behaviour

  • Toasts stack bottom-right in a 24rem column from 640px; below it they span the width with 16px margins and sit above the tab bar.
  • Banners are full width under the top bar at every size; below 640px the action wraps under the text, left-aligned.
  • The notification centre is a single column card at every width; the bell icon in the top bar is the only entry on phones (the tab bar has no bell).

Accessibility

Pattern and roles

The toast region is one persistent role="status" aria-live="polite" container; error toasts and danger banners use role="alert" (APG alert pattern). Toasts are not dialogs and never take focus. The centre is a list of links.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabReaches toast actions and Dismiss in visual order, top to bottom, after the page content that was focused.
Esc inside a toastDismisses it.
EnterActivates the toast action (Undo, Open conversation) or opens a notification.
Focus or hover on a toastPauses its 8-second timer (WCAG 2.2.1 Timing Adjustable).

Focus

Nothing moves focus. When a toast with focus inside is dismissed, focus returns to where it was before it entered the region.

Labelling and announcements

Toast titles are announced as written, so they must make sense alone: "Couldn’t cancel the request", not "Error". The bell’s name carries the count ("Notifications, 3 unread"); unread items prepend hidden "Unread.". Dismiss buttons are labelled "Dismiss".

Motion

Toasts enter and leave with a fade over --duration-base; reduced motion removes the movement. The progress bar is decorative and does not animate under reduced motion.

Touch

Toast close buttons are 24px minimum (--target-min) with 12px gap from the action; the bell is a 44px icon button.

Content

  • Lead with the actor and the event: "Legacy Financial confirmed your meeting", "New message from WaterWorks Plumbing & Drains".
  • The second line carries the detail that makes it actionable: the time, the quote, the item and pickup day.
  • Toast titles are four to six words; the toast text one sentence; the action one or two words (Undo, View in shop, Try again).
  • Banners start with the condition in bold, then what it means: "You’re offline. Showing the directory saved at 8:42 a.m."
  • Request outcomes use the lifecycle words consistently: requested, confirmed, declined, cancelled, completed. Never "approved" for a request (that word belongs to listings) and never "paid".
