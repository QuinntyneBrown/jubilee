Divider

A divider is a hairline rule that separates groups of content without adding a surface. Jubilee mostly separates with spacing and the borders built into rows, cards and dialog footers; the standalone divider appears as the "or use email" rule on the Log in and Sign up screens.

Overview

Reach for spacing first and a divider second. Use a horizontal divider to split long content into groups where headings would be too heavy, a vertical divider between groups of controls in a toolbar, and the text divider between two alternative ways to do the same thing (the Google button, then "or use email" above the form). Rows, Card footers, Dialog footers and table rows draw their own rules; do not add dividers to them.

  • Horizontal: <hr> or .divider, with --space-6 above and below.
  • Vertical: .divider--vertical inside a flex row; kit only, unused in the mocks.
  • Text: .divider-text, a short phrase between two rules.

Anatomy

  1. RulesHairline (--border-width-hairline) in --color-border-default, flexing to fill the row.
  2. Label (text divider)--text-body-sm in --color-fg-muted, --space-4 from each rule. The divider spans its container: the paragraph measure does not apply to it.

Sizes

One weight: a hairline rule. Spacing around a horizontal divider is --space-6; change spacing with layout, not with a thicker rule.

States

Dividers are static decoration with no interactive states.

Responsive behaviour

  • Horizontal and text dividers stretch with their container at every width.
  • A vertical divider stretches to the row's height; when the row wraps on a phone, drop the vertical divider rather than leave it dangling at a line end.

Theming

The rule is --color-border-default in both themes: a warm grey on cream, a soft line on charcoal. It is decorative and does not need 3:1; the text divider's word does need 4.5:1 and gets it from --color-fg-muted.

Accessibility

Role and pattern

<hr> has the implicit separator role; use it where the break is meaningful. Decorative vertical rules are aria-hidden="true"; a meaningful one is role="separator" aria-orientation="vertical". See the APG structural roles guidance.

Keyboard

KeyAction
NoneDividers are not focusable and have no keyboard interaction.

Focus

Never focusable.

Labelling

A separator needs no name. The text divider's words ("or use email") are real text and are read in order, between the Google button and the first field, which is what makes the alternative clear to screen-reader users.

Measured with the contrast checker: the words are 8.10:1 on the surface and 7.23:1 on the canvas in light, 9.35:1 and 10.17:1 in dark.

Motion

None.

Touch

Not a target. Keep at least --space-2 between a vertical divider and the buttons either side so their 24px targets do not touch.

Content

  • The text divider reads "or use email": lowercase, no full stop, and it names what comes next. A bare "or" does not say what the other way is.
  • It appears only with the Google button. When Google sign-in is not configured, neither is shown (L2-003).
  • Do not label a horizontal divider; if the group needs a name, use a heading instead.
