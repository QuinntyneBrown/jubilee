Auth layout

The auth layout is the frame of the bare pages, the ones a visitor meets without the top bar: from Log in and Sign up to the offline page. It has two looks: one centred card, and a split frame that puts an ink brand panel beside the form.

Overview

Use the auth layout for a page that has to work before the visitor has an account or when the app shell cannot be trusted to load: getting in, recovering a password, opening a link from an e-mail, and the system pages. Every other page sits in the shell with the Top bar; see Navigation and page structure.

  • Default (.auth-layout): the brand link, one centred card of at most 26rem and, where the page has one, a line under it. Nine pages use it: join agreement, forgot password, reset password, verify e-mail, confirm e-mail change, maintenance, offline, server error and session expired.
  • Split (.auth-layout--split): Log in and Sign up only. Under 1024px the Lockup sits above a card; from 1024px an ink brand panel takes 5/12 of the screen and the form sits beside it with no card around it (L2-187).

The split frame is drawn from the Magic Patterns design, which shows those two screens only. The other ten pages are not redesigned: do not move one to the split frame until the design shows it (ADR 0038).

Inside the card the two screens compose the Google Button, the text Divider, form fields with the password Text field, a Checkbox and the primary button; their order is in Form layout. The link under the form is documented here.

Anatomy

  1. BrandThe mark and the wordmark as a link to the welcome page: a.auth-brand here, the Lockup in the split frame.
  2. Card.auth-card: with .card in the default frame; in the split frame it draws its own edge under 1024px and none from 1024px.
  3. HeadingThe page's one h1 (h2 in these docs) and a sentence under it; in the split frame .auth-card__head holds .auth-card__title and .auth-card__lead.
  4. ContentA form, or an Empty state for loading, error and success.
  5. Footp.auth-foot: one muted line with the link to the neighbouring page.

Responsive behaviour

The split frame has one switch, at 1024px (--layout-breakpoint-lg), and one easing of the card at 640px.

  • 360px. No panel. The large lockup is centred --space-10 from the top; the card sits --space-10 under it and fills the width less --space-5 on each side, with --space-8 / --space-6 of padding. The link to the other screen is --space-6 under the card, and --space-16 closes the page. Every field is on its own row.
  • 768px. The phone layout with more room, as the design's own 640px step: from 640px the lockup starts --space-16 from the top, the card's padding is --space-10 and its width stops at 28.75rem. On Sign up, First name and Last name sit side by side.
  • 1024px, the switch. The frame becomes a grid of 5fr 7fr, exactly as tall as the viewport. The brand panel appears on the left; the lockup above the card goes, because the panel carries one. The card loses its surface, edge, corners and shadow, and widens to 32.5rem. The form side centres the card and scrolls on its own when the form is taller than the screen. On Sign up, Email and Password sit side by side.
  • 1280px. The same layout at its fullest: the panel's padding and headline grow with the viewport from 1024px and stop here, at --space-16 and --font-size-5xl.
  • Default frame. Centred at every width: the card is 26rem at most and --layout-margin from the screen's edges.

The live examples on this page follow the window, not their own width: narrow the window under 1024px to see the card. From 1024px they are a screen 800px tall and at least 1280px wide, drawn at 68%; open the mocks for the real size.

Theming

The panel is --color-bg-brand with --color-fg-on-brand text: ink in both themes, like the top bar and the hero band. Only the form side follows the theme. Under 1024px the page is --color-bg-canvas and the card --color-bg-surface; from 1024px the whole form side is --color-bg-surface. The frame reads tokens only and none were added for it (ADR 0038).

Accessibility

Role and pattern

The frame is the page's <main> landmark. The brand panel is an <aside> named “About Jubilee”, so a screen-reader user can skip it or find it. Each screen has exactly one <h1>: the title of the card, or the title of the empty state in the error and success states, or a visually hidden one while loading. The panel's headline is a paragraph, not a heading: it is the same slogan on both screens and must not compete with the title of the task. See the APG landmark regions practice.

Keyboard

KeyAction
TabSkip link, then the lockup, then the form from top to bottom, then the link under the form. On Log in: Continue with Google, Email, Forgot password?, Password, Show password, Keep me logged in, Log in, Create a free account.
EnterFollows the focused link or presses the focused button; in a field it submits the form.
SpacePresses the focused button, Show password included, and ticks the checkbox.

Focus

Only one lockup is ever in the tab order: the one on the panel from 1024px, the one above the card below that; the other is not rendered. After a failed submit focus moves to the error summary. Every stop shows the shared 2px ring. On the ink panel the ring must be --color-focus-ring-on-brand, as on every brand surface, because the light theme's ink ring measures 1.00:1 there; the kit does not yet say so for the panel (see Sources).

Labelling

The lockup link is named “Jubilee home” by its aria-label; its mark is hidden. The h1 names the task. The link under the form makes sense alone: “Create a free account”, “Log in”.

The gold underline of the link measures 1.73:1 on the surface and 1.55:1 on the page in light (10.25:1 and 11.15:1 in dark). It does not need 3:1, because it is not what tells the reader this is a link: the link is also semibold and in the text colour, 19.32:1 on the surface and 17.25:1 on the page, inside a muted sentence.

Motion

None. The switch at 1024px is a change of layout, not an animation.

Touch

Under 1024px the lockup is a link about 50px tall and every control is 44px or 48px tall. The link under the form is a link in a sentence, which the 24px target rule exempts (2.5.8).

Content

  • The two screens take the design's words exactly. Log in: “Welcome back”, “Log in to message businesses and manage your listing.” Sign up: “Join Jubilee”, “Free for everyone in Toronto’s Christian community.”
  • The panel says the same on both screens: “Hire, shop and book with your church family.”, “Christian-owned businesses across Toronto, all in one place.” and “© Jubilee · Toronto”.
  • The line under the form is a question and its answer: “New to Jubilee? Create a free account”, “Already on Jubilee? Log in”. The question is muted; the link names the action.
  • In the card the order is fixed: heading, a notice or the error summary if there is one, the Google button, “or use email”, the form.
  • These two screens say “Log in” and “Email”. The top bar, the sign-in-required dialog, the session-expired page, the welcome page and every e-mail still say “Sign in”, “Join free” and “E-mail”, and are left for one later change of vocabulary across the product (ADR 0038). Do not rename them here; the routes stay /sign-in and /join.
  • A default-frame page has one heading that says what the page is for, one sentence, and one primary action.
