Stars

Stars show a rating someone gave or the average of many: five stars, each filled to its share of the value, so 4.6 fills six tenths of the fifth. They are read, not pressed; to ask for a rating use the Star scale.

Overview

  • One role="img" named in words: “Rated 4.6 out of 5” for an average, “4 out of 5 stars” for one review. The stars inside are decoration.
  • Where text beside them already says the rating (the Rating link), the stars are aria-hidden and carry no name.
  • Each star is a track star with a gold star over it, clipped by .stars__fill to its share; the width is the only inline style.

Anatomy

  1. Host.stars, role="img" with the rating in words.
  2. Track star.stars__track: the empty star, --color-bg-subtle-hover.
  3. Fill.stars__fill clips a .stars__glyph in --color-accent to the star’s share.

States

Stars have no interactive state: they are never focused, hovered or pressed. A list that is loading draws a skeleton in their place.

Responsive behaviour

  • The stars never shrink or wrap; five small stars are 83px, five large 116px.
  • The size is set by the context, not the viewport: small in a review, default under a name and in the summary, large on the posted screen.

Theming

The fill is --color-accent; the track is --color-bg-subtle-hover, a step darker than the subtle ground in light and lighter in dark. In forced colours the track is GrayText and the fill CanvasText.

Both themes

Accessibility

Role and pattern

role="img" with aria-label: one name for the five stars. Never colour alone: the number is said in the name and, in every use, in visible text beside the stars (L2-160).

Contrast

Fill on the page
Track on the page
Fill on the page
Track on the page

The stars are not the only carrier of the value: the average or the review’s own text is beside them, so their graphic contrast is decorative.

Content

  • An average: “Rated {average} out of 5”, the average to one decimal (“4.6”).
  • One review: “{n} out of 5 stars”.
